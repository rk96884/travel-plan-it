# Travel Plan It — Marketing Strategy

**Status:** Working strategy  
**Owner:** Travel Plan It LTD  
**Planning horizon:** Pre-launch through Year 1  
**Year-1 target:** £200,000–£250,000 total booking value (TTV)

## 1. Purpose

This document is the source of truth for Travel Plan It's initial marketing approach. The strategy is intentionally lean: validate demand and acquisition channels before committing significant fixed cost or paid-media budget.

The working Year-1 commercial model is approximately **50 bookings at an average £5,000 booking value = £250,000 TTV**.

## 2. Positioning

Travel Plan It will be a consultant-led, predominantly online travel business specialising initially in tailor-made long-haul travel, with particular strength in Asia and complex multi-component itineraries.

The proposition should combine:

- genuine itinerary-design expertise;
- human advice for trips that are difficult to assemble well through a conventional OTA;
- convenient digital discovery and enquiry;
- transparent, responsive service;
- specialist supplier and consolidator relationships available through the chosen host/membership structure.

The brand should not compete primarily on being the cheapest. Marketing should demonstrate why expert itinerary design creates value.

## 3. Lean marketing principles

1. **Low fixed cost first.** Avoid building a large permanent marketing stack before demand is validated.
2. **Test before scaling.** Run small experiments and increase investment only when there is evidence of qualified demand.
3. **Expertise supplies the substance; AI supplies leverage.** AI may accelerate research, scripting, creative production, variants and analysis, but destination and itinerary claims require human review.
4. **Reuse high-value inputs.** One itinerary should be capable of becoming a landing page, article, email, short-form videos, social posts and paid-ad variants.
5. **Organic before paid amplification where practical.** Use organic creative testing to identify promising hooks and formats before committing larger media budgets.
6. **Measure commercial outcomes.** Optimise for qualified enquiries, quotes, bookings, CAC and contribution rather than reach or followers alone.
7. **Remain tool-agnostic.** Tools such as ChatGPT/OpenAI, Creatify and Zeely are production options, not dependencies.

## 4. Growth engines

### 4.1 B2C

Initial B2C acquisition should combine:

- search/SEO around destinations, itineraries and complex-trip planning;
- Google Search for high-intent queries;
- Instagram, Facebook, TikTok and YouTube Shorts for discovery;
- AI-assisted short-form video;
- email/CRM nurturing;
- referrals and repeat business;
- partnerships with relevant lifestyle, concierge and HNW-facing organisations where commercially viable.

### 4.2 B2B

A second growth engine should test whether Travel Plan It can provide itinerary research, construction and pricing support to:

- independent travel consultants;
- concierge/lifestyle businesses;
- travel professionals requiring specialist long-haul/Asia expertise;
- other partners that retain the end-client relationship but require fulfilment expertise.

The commercial and regulatory structure for B2B fulfilment must be confirmed with the selected host/membership arrangement before launch.

## 5. Year-1 acquisition model

The initial planning target is:

| Metric | Working assumption |
| --- | ---: |
| Total booking value | £250,000 |
| Average booking value | £5,000 |
| Required bookings | 50 |
| Average monthly bookings across 12 months | 4.2 |

The strategy will not assume that 50 bookings simply appear. A separate acquisition forecast will model:

**Traffic / audience → enquiry → qualified enquiry → proposal / quote → booking → TTV → gross margin → acquisition cost**

Three scenarios will be maintained:

- Conservative
- Base
- Stretch

Conversion assumptions must initially be treated as hypotheses and replaced with actual Travel Plan It data as soon as sufficient volume exists.

## 6. AI-assisted content and video engine

Travel Plan It will test an AI-assisted creative workflow designed to produce more high-quality marketing assets without creating an expensive internal production operation.

### Working production flow

**Travel opportunity → expert angle → hook/script → storyboard/assets → AI-assisted production → human accuracy/brand review → organic test → measurement → paid amplification of winners**

Candidate production routes include:

- **ChatGPT/OpenAI** — ideation, research support, hooks, scripts, storyboards, imagery/creative assets and available video-generation workflows;
- **Creatify** — purpose-built performance-ad/video production;
- **Zeely AI** — AI-assisted video/ad creative and campaign-oriented workflows;
- conventional editing or creator-produced footage where AI is not the best solution.

No paid AI-video platform should become a permanent cost until it demonstrates useful output.

### Initial AI video experiment

Create equivalent Travel Plan It concepts using available production routes and compare:

- cost per usable creative;
- production time;
- visual quality;
- destination authenticity;
- brand consistency;
- ability to edit;
- 9:16 short-form suitability;
- voice/avatar quality where used;
- platform suitability;
- engagement;
- qualified-enquiry performance once campaigns are live.

Initial prototype themes should include:

1. Thailand / multi-centre itinerary;
2. Japan itinerary;
3. complex multi-country Asia itinerary.

Example creative angles:

- itinerary mistakes to avoid;
- how an expert would structure a multi-centre trip;
- what a given budget can realistically buy;
- destination combinations that work well;
- why route/order matters;
- expert itinerary breakdowns.

AI-generated content must not invent hotel availability, pricing, flight schedules, entry requirements or other time-sensitive travel facts.

## 7. Content flywheel

A core itinerary or destination concept should be designed for reuse:

**Itinerary research**
→ destination/itinerary page  
→ SEO article  
→ 3–10 short-form video concepts  
→ social posts  
→ email content  
→ paid-search/social landing page  
→ remarketing creative

This reduces the marginal cost of content production and keeps marketing aligned with products Travel Plan It can actually sell.

## 8. Channel prioritisation

### Pre-launch

Focus on foundations and learning:

- positioning and messaging;
- measurement architecture;
- SEO/site architecture;
- initial destination/itinerary content;
- prototype video production;
- social account foundations;
- CRM/email foundations;
- partnership prospecting.

### Launch

Activate controlled tests across high-intent search, organic social/video, selected paid social, email capture/nurture, referrals and B2B outreach.

### Scale

Increase investment only where channel economics and lead quality justify it. Winning creative should be iterated rather than continually replaced without learning.

## 9. Measurement framework

At minimum, measure:

- website sessions by source;
- enquiry starts and completed enquiries;
- cost per enquiry;
- qualified-enquiry rate;
- quote/proposal rate;
- booking conversion;
- average booking value;
- total booking value;
- gross margin / commission generated;
- customer acquisition cost;
- contribution after acquisition cost;
- creative-level performance;
- lead-to-booking time;
- repeat/referral contribution.

A marketing channel should not be considered successful solely because it generates cheap leads.

## 10. TNG/TTA membership relevance

Before the final membership application, this strategy should provide a credible explanation of how Travel Plan It intends to reach its Year-1 booking target.

The application/business plan can summarise:

- target market and specialist proposition;
- founders' complementary travel and digital/customer-acquisition experience;
- £200k–£250k Year-1 TTV objective;
- B2C and B2B acquisition routes;
- lean AI-assisted content production;
- controlled paid-media testing;
- measurement and scaling discipline.

Supplier access, consumer protection, booking/payment processes, branding permissions and commercial terms must reflect the final TNG/TTA arrangement rather than assumptions in marketing material.
