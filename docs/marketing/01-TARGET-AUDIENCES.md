# Travel Plan It — Target Audiences

**Status:** Working strategy  
**Parent:** `00-MARKETING-STRATEGY.md`

## 1. Purpose

Define the audiences Travel Plan It should test first, while keeping Year-1 marketing focused enough to support a £200k–£250k TTV target.

## 2. Audience prioritisation principle

Travel Plan It should not launch as a generic "holidays for everyone" agency. Early marketing should favour customers whose trips benefit from itinerary expertise and whose likely booking value can support consultant time and acquisition cost.

Priority is based on:

- fit with tailor-made long-haul and Asia expertise;
- complexity of trip;
- likely booking value and margin opportunity;
- identifiable search/social intent;
- need for human advice;
- repeat/referral potential;
- ability to reach the audience efficiently.

## 3. Primary B2C audiences

### A. Complex long-haul planners

**Need:** A multi-stop or multi-component long-haul trip that is difficult to assemble confidently.

Typical characteristics:

- couples, families or small groups;
- researching several destinations/hotels/transport options;
- willing to pay for the right itinerary rather than simply the cheapest component price;
- high research burden and fear of getting routing/timing wrong.

**TPI value:** Turn a complicated idea into a coherent, bookable itinerary.

**Potential content:** route comparisons, itinerary breakdowns, planning mistakes, "how many nights?" guidance, realistic budget examples.

### B. Asia-first explorers

**Need:** Specialist help designing a first or more ambitious Asia trip.

Initial destination themes may include Thailand, Japan and multi-country combinations, subject to commercial/supplier validation.

**TPI value:** Destination knowledge plus expert sequencing of flights, hotels, transfers and experiences.

**Potential content:** two-week itineraries, city/beach combinations, first-timer guides, seasonal planning and multi-country routes.

### C. Time-poor experience seekers

**Need:** A high-quality trip without spending evenings comparing dozens of options.

**TPI value:** Consultant-led research and itinerary construction, supported by an efficient digital enquiry journey.

Marketing should emphasise time saved, confidence and thoughtful design rather than "luxury" as a blanket label.

### D. Milestone and special-trip customers

Examples include honeymoons, anniversaries, major birthdays and once-in-a-lifetime trips.

These customers may have stronger emotional stakes, higher research needs and greater demand for reassurance.

TPI should test this segment rather than assume it automatically converts better.

## 4. Secondary B2C audiences

### Lifestyle / concierge / HNW-referred customers

Rather than attempting to acquire all affluent customers directly through expensive advertising, TPI should investigate relationships with:

- concierge and lifestyle businesses;
- personal assistants / executive assistants where appropriate;
- premium service businesses with complementary customers;
- professional/referral networks.

This is initially a partnership experiment, not a core revenue assumption.

## 5. B2B audiences

### A. Independent travel consultants

**Problem:** They own the client relationship but may lack time or specialist expertise to research and construct complex Asia/long-haul itineraries.

**Potential TPI role:** Trade-facing itinerary research, construction, pricing and/or fulfilment subject to the selected host's rules.

### B. Concierge and lifestyle businesses

**Problem:** Client requests may fall outside internal travel expertise.

**Potential TPI role:** Specialist travel partner or fulfilment resource, with commercial/referral structure agreed in advance.

### C. Other travel professionals

Test selectively where TPI's specialist knowledge fills a genuine capability gap.

## 6. Non-priority launch audiences

Avoid spending meaningful Year-1 budget pursuing:

- commodity short-haul city breaks;
- flight-only bargain hunters;
- customers whose principal decision criterion is lowest price;
- very low-value bookings requiring disproportionate consultant effort;
- broad untargeted "luxury travel" audiences without evidence of fit.

This does not mean refusing such enquiries; it means not designing the acquisition strategy around them.

## 7. Initial audience hypotheses

| Audience | Problem intensity | TPI expertise fit | Expected booking value | Initial priority |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Complex long-haul planners | High | High | High | Primary |
| Asia-first explorers | High | High | Medium–High | Primary |
| Time-poor experience seekers | Medium–High | High | High | Primary |
| Milestone/special trips | High | High | High | Primary test |
| Concierge/HNW referrals | High | Medium–High | High | Partnership test |
| Independent consultants | High | High | Varies | B2B test |
| Commodity short-haul | Low | Low | Low | Low |

"Expected" values above are hypotheses and must be validated against actual lead and booking data.

## 8. Data to capture from launch

For every enquiry where practical:

- acquisition source/campaign;
- destination(s);
- trip type;
- party size;
- approximate budget;
- intended travel date;
- complexity/multi-centre requirement;
- qualified/unqualified status;
- quoted value;
- booked value;
- gross margin/commission;
- reason won/lost where known.

This will allow TPI to replace personas with evidence.

## 9. Next validation work

- Interview/observe likely customers and travel consultants.
- Analyse search intent around initial Asia itinerary themes.
- Test 3–5 creative hooks per primary B2C audience.
- Run initial B2B discovery conversations.
- Review booking economics by segment after the first meaningful sample.
