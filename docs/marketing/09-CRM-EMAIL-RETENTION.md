# Travel Plan It — CRM, Email and Retention Strategy

**Status:** Working strategy  
**Parent:** `00-MARKETING-STRATEGY.md`

## 1. Objective

Use CRM and email to prevent valuable enquiries from being lost, support longer travel decision cycles and create repeat/referral value without unnecessary automation complexity.

## 2. Principle

Automation should improve responsiveness while preserving a human consultancy experience.

## 3. Minimum customer lifecycle

**Visitor → enquiry → qualified → consultation/research → proposal/quote → booked → travelled → review/referral/repeat**

Lost/deferred opportunities should have explicit statuses rather than disappearing from the pipeline.

## 4. Enquiry capture

Capture only information useful for qualification/planning, potentially:

- name/contact details;
- destination(s);
- intended dates/flexibility;
- party;
- approximate budget;
- trip type;
- initial requirements;
- marketing source/UTM;
- marketing consent separately where required.

The form should balance useful qualification with completion friction.

## 5. Operational email flows

Potential flows:

### Enquiry acknowledgement

Immediate confirmation that the request has been received, with realistic next-step expectations.

### Human follow-up

Prompt personalised contact for qualified opportunities.

### Proposal/quote follow-up

Structured reminders/tasks rather than relying on memory.

### Deferred travel

Where a customer is not ready, capture an appropriate future follow-up date.

### Pre/post travel

Operational communications should be separated conceptually from marketing consent. After travel, request feedback/review and offer an appropriate route back for future planning.

## 6. Marketing nurture

Useful themes:

- itinerary inspiration;
- planning guidance;
- destination timing;
- new route ideas;
- relevant content based on expressed interest.

Avoid sending high-frequency generic promotional email without evidence it helps.

## 7. Segmentation

Start simple:

- destination/region interest;
- booked vs enquiry;
- qualified vs unqualified;
- travel timing;
- B2C vs B2B;
- source/channel.

Add complexity only when it creates a clear action.

## 8. Referral and repeat

After successful travel:

- ask for feedback/review;
- make referral easy;
- retain appropriate destination/preferences context subject to privacy rules;
- create a future-trip reactivation process.

Do not assume repeat travel will materially support Year-1 acquisition; measure it.

## 9. Measurement

- enquiry response time;
- qualification rate;
- proposal follow-up completion;
- email-assisted conversion;
- reactivated opportunities;
- repeat bookings;
- referral bookings;
- unsubscribe/complaint rate;
- revenue/margin influenced.

## 10. Data protection

CRM/email implementation must follow applicable UK data-protection and electronic-marketing requirements, including appropriate lawful basis/consent handling, privacy information, retention controls and unsubscribe/suppression processes.

Specific legal/technical controls should be documented in the appropriate compliance/architecture documentation rather than invented in marketing copy.
