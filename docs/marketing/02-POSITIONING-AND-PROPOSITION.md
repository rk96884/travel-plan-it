# Travel Plan It — Positioning and Proposition

**Status:** Working strategy  
**Parent:** `00-MARKETING-STRATEGY.md`

## 1. Positioning objective

Travel Plan It should occupy the space between self-service online booking and traditional high-touch travel consultancy:

> **Expertly planned, tailor-made travel for trips that deserve more thought than a booking engine can give them.**

This is a working positioning line, not approved final advertising copy.

## 2. Core proposition

Travel Plan It helps customers turn complex long-haul travel ideas into coherent tailor-made itineraries, with Asia as an initial specialist strength.

The value is not simply access to flights and hotels. It is:

- itinerary design;
- route and sequencing expertise;
- research reduction;
- specialist advice;
- coordination of multiple components;
- human judgement;
- a convenient online-led customer experience.

## 3. Why customers should care

### Complexity reduced

Multi-centre long-haul travel can involve flights, transfers, hotels, internal transport, timing and destination trade-offs. TPI should make the whole trip make sense.

### Time returned

Customers should not need to spend dozens of evenings comparing tabs, reviews and routing options.

### Better-informed decisions

TPI can explain why an itinerary is structured a particular way, rather than presenting an unexplained package.

### Human support

The proposition should retain access to a knowledgeable person while using technology to make discovery and communication efficient.

## 4. Differentiation

TPI should avoid generic claims such as "unforgettable holidays" unless supported by something more specific.

Initial differentiation should come from the combination of:

1. **Complex itinerary expertise** — trips where sequencing and component choice matter.
2. **Asia specialism** — an initial area of demonstrable expertise, without restricting the company to Asia.
3. **Consultant + digital model** — expert human input with modern online acquisition and communication.
4. **Efficient content engine** — useful itinerary-led marketing that demonstrates expertise before the customer enquires.
5. **Potential trade capability** — specialist itinerary support for other travel professionals, subject to host approval.

## 5. Brand personality

Marketing should feel:

- knowledgeable, not superior;
- inspiring, but practical;
- premium in care, not unnecessarily exclusive;
- modern and digitally fluent;
- clear about what is known versus indicative;
- human even when AI assists production.

Avoid a generic AI-travel aesthetic or language that makes TPI appear to be an automated itinerary generator.

## 6. Message architecture

### Master message

**You bring the travel idea. We make the itinerary work.**

Working copy for testing only.

### Supporting messages

**For complexity:**  
Multi-centre and long-haul trips designed as one journey, not a collection of disconnected bookings.

**For expertise:**  
Specialist itinerary planning with particular strength in Asia.

**For time:**  
Spend less time comparing options and more time looking forward to the trip.

**For confidence:**  
Understand why your route, stops and components have been chosen.

## 7. Evidence strategy

Claims should increasingly be supported by:

- real itinerary examples;
- destination expertise;
- consultant/team experience;
- customer reviews once available;
- transparent process explanations;
- relevant memberships/protection once formally confirmed;
- useful planning content.

Do not advertise host, ABTA, ATOL or other protection/affiliation claims until wording and permissions are confirmed under the final membership arrangement.

## 8. Offer architecture

The primary B2C call to action should initially be **enquiry / trip consultation**, not an attempt to force complex travel into instant checkout.

A useful enquiry journey should capture enough information to qualify the opportunity without creating a burdensome form.

Potential entry offers/content:

- "Plan my trip" enquiry;
- sample itineraries;
- destination/route guides;
- budget-led itinerary examples;
- downloadable planning guides where lead capture adds value.

## 9. B2B proposition — working hypothesis

> **Specialist long-haul itinerary support for travel professionals who want to retain their client relationship.**

Possible services could include itinerary research, design, pricing or fulfilment. The exact proposition must follow the commercial, licensing and consumer-protection rules of TPI's eventual host/membership arrangement.

## 10. Messaging tests

Test materially different value propositions rather than tiny wording changes:

1. **Expertise-led:** "Asia and complex long-haul, planned by people who know how the journey fits together."
2. **Time-led:** "Tell us the trip you have in mind. We'll do the hard part."
3. **Complexity-led:** "Five stops. Three flights. One itinerary that actually works."
4. **Budget/value-led:** "What can £5,000 buy for two weeks in Thailand?" — only where figures are current, clearly indicative and verified.

## 11. Success criteria

Positioning is working when it improves commercial signals such as:

- qualified enquiry rate;
- enquiry-to-consultation/quote progression;
- booking conversion;
- average booking value;
- margin;
- customer feedback indicating clear understanding of TPI's value.

Engagement alone is insufficient.
