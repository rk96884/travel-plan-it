# Travel Plan It — Year-1 Acquisition Forecast

**Status:** Planning model / assumptions require validation  
**Parent:** `00-MARKETING-STRATEGY.md`

## 1. Commercial target

The current Year-1 working target is:

- **£200,000–£250,000 TTV**
- Base planning case: **£250,000 TTV**
- Working average booking value (ABV): **£5,000**
- Required base-case bookings: **50**

This document reverse-engineers the customer-acquisition requirement. It is not a forecast of guaranteed performance.

## 2. Funnel definitions

**Lead / enquiry:** A person submits a meaningful trip enquiry.

**Qualified enquiry:** The enquiry is within TPI's service scope and has credible timing, budget and intent.

**Proposal / quote:** TPI progresses the opportunity to a priced or sufficiently developed itinerary/proposal.

**Booking:** Customer commits and the booking is confirmed under the applicable booking process.

## 3. Base-case funnel hypothesis

Until TPI has real conversion data, use explicit assumptions rather than false precision.

A useful starting hypothesis is:

| Funnel stage | Base assumption |
| --- | ---: |
| Enquiries | 250 |
| Qualified enquiry rate | 60% |
| Qualified enquiries | 150 |
| Qualified → booking conversion | 33.3% |
| Bookings | 50 |
| Average booking value | £5,000 |
| TTV | £250,000 |

This means roughly **5 raw enquiries per booking** in the base planning case.

The 33.3% qualified-to-booking assumption is deliberately a test assumption, not an industry benchmark.

## 5. Monthly pacing

A straight-line base case would require approximately:

- 21 enquiries/month;
- 12–13 qualified enquiries/month;
- 4–5 bookings/month;
- ~£20,833 TTV/month.

Travel demand is seasonal, and a startup will ramp rather than perform evenly. Therefore straight-line pacing should be used only as an annual sanity check.

## 6. Illustrative channel contribution

Do not commit the entire target to paid advertising. A diversified base-case hypothesis could be:

| Acquisition source | Illustrative bookings | TTV at £5k ABV |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Organic search/content | 12 | £60,000 |
| Paid search | 10 | £50,000 |
| Organic social/video | 6 | £30,000 |
| Paid social/video | 6 | £30,000 |
| Referral/network | 6 | £30,000 |
| Partnerships/B2B-derived B2C | 5 | £25,000 |
| Email/CRM/re-engagement | 5 | £25,000 |
| **Total** | **50** | **£250,000** |

This is a planning allocation only. Channels should earn budget based on observed economics.

B2B fulfilment revenue may need separate commercial metrics rather than being forced into the same £5k B2C booking model.

## 7. CAC framework

TPI should distinguish:

**Media CAC** = paid media spend / bookings attributable to that media.

**Blended CAC** = total attributable marketing spend / total new customers.

**Contribution after acquisition** should be monitored using actual gross margin/commission rather than TTV.

Example only:

If a £5,000 booking generates an 18% gross margin, gross margin before relevant transaction/host/operating costs would be:

**£5,000 × 18% = £900**

CAC must be judged against the actual retained economics of each booking, not against the £5,000 headline booking value.

## 8. Paid-media guardrails

At launch:

- use small controlled budgets;
- separate brand, destination and high-intent search where practical;
- do not scale from click-through rate alone;
- assess lead quality and downstream booking behaviour;
- pause or revise channels producing cheap but poor-quality enquiries;
- calculate allowable CAC from retained margin once real booking economics are known.

A formal allowable-CAC ceiling should be set only after TNG/TTA/host costs, merchant fees and expected margin retention are confirmed.

## 9. AI-video economics

For each AI creative route — ChatGPT/OpenAI, Creatify, Zeely or another workflow — record:

- subscription/credit cost;
- human production/review time;
- number of generated variants;
- number of usable variants;
- organic performance;
- paid spend where applicable;
- enquiries;
- qualified enquiries;
- bookings;
- cost per usable creative;
- cost per qualified enquiry;
- CAC.

The cheapest tool is not necessarily the leanest if it produces unusable or low-trust creative.

## 10. Early warning indicators

Review strategy if:

- qualified enquiry rate is materially below assumptions;
- average booking value is materially below the £5k working assumption;
- consultant time per enquiry makes low-value leads uneconomic;
- quote-to-booking conversion is weak;
- paid CAC approaches or exceeds sustainable retained contribution;
- particular audiences consistently produce low-quality demand.

## 11. Data maturity

### Stage 0 — pre-launch

All conversion figures are hypotheses.

### Stage 1 — first 25–50 enquiries

Look for obvious qualification and channel-quality patterns; avoid overinterpreting small samples.

### Stage 2 — first 10–20 bookings

Begin replacing ABV, conversion and margin assumptions with TPI observations.

### Stage 3 — meaningful Year-1 sample

Use cohort/channel economics for budget allocation and Year-2 planning.

## 12. Membership application use

For TNG/TTA discussions, the important point is not to claim that a specific funnel conversion is known. The stronger explanation is:

- TPI has a £200k–£250k Year-1 target;
- the base case equates to about 50 £5k bookings;
- the target has been reverse-engineered into enquiry requirements;
- acquisition is diversified across organic, paid, referral, CRM and partnership channels;
- spend will be scaled against measured lead quality, CAC and retained margin;
- the founders bring complementary travel expertise and digital/customer-acquisition capability.

This demonstrates a controlled route to the target while remaining transparent about startup-stage assumptions.
